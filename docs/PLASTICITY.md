**Permanent deformation — frames with retained edge lengths**

Objects now choose one of two responses. **Deformable** objects retain their connected frame and change its rest shape under sufficient load. **Destructible** objects keep the existing rigid-chunk fracture model. There is no combined bending-and-tearing mode yet. Rocks and walls remain destructible; both barrels and the new blue metal fence are deformable.

Open **O → L** for starters; **MORE STARTERS** shows the fence. In **MATERIAL**, deformable objects have a **YIELD & HARDENING** shortcut. Any object can change mode through **OBJECT BEHAVIOR → PERMANENT DEFORMATION**. This uses the authored triangulated mesh as a welded deformation frame; it does not yet offer a separate beam/shell authoring tool. A pinned edge stays fixed in deformable mode. Shape, mesh detail, mass and friction remain editable.

| Control | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Yield stress, kPa | Effective stress needed to begin permanent deformation; 0.1–1,000,000. These are tunable effective object properties, not measured steel yield values. |
| Flow rate, /s | Rate of strain change above yield; 0.01–20. Higher values deform faster. |
| Hardening | Dimensionless increase in resistance with accumulated strain; 0–100. Zero gives constant yield resistance. |
| Strain limit | Maximum accumulated equivalent strain per cell; 0.02–3. At the limit the frame stops deforming rather than tearing. |
| Shell resistance × | Multiplies plastic yield and work for shell/blast loads. |
| Crushable / crush force | Require sufficiently strong opposed contact loads before contact can deform the frame. Enabled on barrels, disabled on the fence. Shell loads do not require pinning. |

Contact resistance, crush scale, seam failure strengths, Gc and runtime refinement only control destructible behavior. The editor hides those behavior controls for deformable objects; the recipe retains their values if you switch back. Young's modulus still supplies the structural stiffness distribution. Uniformly changing E need not visibly change this quasistatic response.

**How it works**

Each frame remains one Rapier body between permanent shape updates, so there are no soft dynamic joints or visible elastic wobble. The existing equilibrium solve supplies interface forces and moments. Equivalent stress combines axial, shear and bending stress. Yield grows as `yield_stress × (1 + hardening × accumulated_strain)`. Overstress supplies a bounded plastic rate, projected through the structural system into compatible cell translations and rotations.

Shared vertices are welded across intact interfaces; support vertices are pinned, and hanging vertices follow their parent edge. Every exterior frame edge retains its original authored length. A bounded nonlinear constraint projection (at most 96 sweeps) redistributes the loaded displacement into bending instead of allowing the rim to shorten. These are rest-shape constraints, not dynamic springs. A horizontally squeezed barrel therefore flattens and widens sideways; its hollow interior is still allowed to lose area. The projection respects welded/support/hanging vertices. A free object is aligned back to its original center and orientation so constraint corrections cannot teleport or rotate it. Rim lengths must match their original values within 0.002%, so tolerances do not accumulate shrinkage across steps. Small candidate steps change the actual triangle vertices, local centers, bond faces, collision shapes and mass properties. Inverted triangles, self-overlap, excessive external penetration and near-zero areas reject a step; backtracking reduces it. Work, the 15 mm step limit and strain limits are checked after rim projection, including the transverse motion. A step moves any vertex by at most 15 mm. Extreme folding can stall at these geometry guards. A single triangle has no internal interface to yield; use a mesh with bonded cells. Start coarse: collision checks and refactorization on each accepted rest update make detailed deformable frames more expensive than static rigid ones.

Plastic work uses reference material volume, yield, hardening and incremental strain. It is charged against the existing impact/contact work budget. A spinning frame preserves angular momentum, and any resulting increase in rotational kinetic energy is charged too. Contact impulses already applied by Rapier are not applied again. Reference mass is preserved even when the visible cross-section compresses. After unloading there is no restoring spring: the altered shape is the new rest shape.

An explosive ignites once on accepted permanent deformation, or on an internal crack for destructible objects. Its vessel is consumed before the occluded blast damages neighbors. Ordinary impacts neither fracture nor adaptively subdivide deformable objects. The [laser](AMMUNITION.md) can explicitly subdivide and cut their frames without force. Reset restores the authored shape; simulation changes never overwrite the editor draft.

This is an effective 2D rigid/plastic approximation, not continuum plastic FEM, incompressible metal, a hollow shell solver or out-of-plane buckling. The filled barrel cross-section represents an effective hollow vessel: its enclosing frame keeps its edge lengths while its interior area may decrease. This is an inextensible frame approximation; axial stretching of metal edges is not modeled. It avoids treating the barrel as either collapsing foam or an incompressible filled bag. No elastic recovery, tearing, fatigue, springback or vertical folding is simulated. Shapes and results remain mesh-, timestep- and contact-dependent. Related rest-shape plasticity ideas appear in [Müller et al., Meshless Deformations Based on Shape Matching, §4.5](https://graphics.stanford.edu/courses/cs468-05-fall/Papers/p471-muller.pdf); this implementation uses the existing interface graph and work budgets, not that paper's shape-matching algorithm.

**Experiments and verification**

[The ten-barrel study](http://127.0.0.1:8000/?experiment=barrels) uses yield columns 0.5, 1, 2, 4 and 8 kPa, and hardening rows 0 and 4. All ten share the same 72 kg body, 4500 kg tank at 0.8 m/s, backstop and four-second duration. Cards report peak accumulated cell strain and plastic work. A new generic comparison of a deformable object defaults to a yield sweep; the inspector also supports flow and hardening sweeps.

[The six deformation-limit cases](http://127.0.0.1:8000/?experiment=crush) compare **0.4 / 0.8 / 1.2 / 1.6 / 2.2 / 3.0** under identical impacts. Strain limit caps accumulated equivalent strain in each cell; it is dimensionless and is **not** the percentage of barrel width lost. Yield sets the onset/resistance, flow sets the rate, and hardening increases resistance with accumulated strain. Actual flattening also depends on force, contact geometry and duration, and may stop at a geometry guard before the limit. The centered native eight-second trial produces widths about **1.020 / 0.910 / 0.830 / 0.761 / 0.698 / 0.636 m**, respectively, from 1.30 m. These are measured results for this recipe, not prescribed shapes.

Shared speed, angle, offset, mass, duration and backstop controls sit above all six views. Edit then RUN ALL in the running application; no compilation is involved. Focus a case to change its own material values. The deformation solver is still the accepted rim-only version. The wall-facing frame can fold inward; no flat/convex contact-side outline is imposed.

The contact-corner constraint and deeper-crush profile introduced in `plastic-frame-contact-3` were removed after the user said **“no, the deformation was better before.”** Current behavior restores the edge-length-only projection from `plastic-frame-rim-2`, while retaining the six-case study and impact controls. The user subsequently selected case 6, limit 3: the ordinary barrel now uses yield 500 Pa, flow 2/s, hardening 0 and limit 3. Generic plasticity and the explosive barrel retain the earlier 1.2 cap. Rim lengths, mass, permanent shape retention and the existing geometry/work guards remain enforced. Mesh and strain/geometry limits can still stop extreme flattening; there is no tearing or remeshing in deformable mode. Saved user material values are not rewritten; load the barrel starter for the accepted case-6 profile, or RUN a saved recipe to replay its own settings.

Recordings retain immutable geometry revisions, so scrubbing back restores the original shape. Saved checkpoints contain the deformed geometry; RUN reconstructs the entire experiment. JSON v1 remains readable: missing `plasticity` means the old destructible behavior; missing plastic metrics mean zero. Current engine revision is `experiment-terrain-bridges-13`. The ordinary barrel now has a speed-dependent contact bounce curve; this does not change permanent rest-shape deformation. See [OBJECT_LIBRARY.md](OBJECT_LIBRARY.md).

Native tests cover transverse expansion under horizontal/vertical/diagonal compression at two mesh resolutions, retained authored rim lengths, insufficient stress/work, strain limits, unchanged shapes after unloading, conserved mass and momentum, work limits, actual collider updates, support attachment, no self-overlap, explosive ignition and exact ten-case replay including geometry. Browser scripts `plasticity-smoke.mjs` and `barrel-smoke.mjs` cover authoring, persistence, tank/fence contact and timeline shape restoration.

The rim constraint was added after the user observed the barrel narrowing across the force. The previous model preserved mass but allowed frame-edge contraction; some variants became shorter transversely as crushing progressed. Width/height measurements are now printed by `cargo run --release -p sim --example barrel_comparison`, and the browser barrel suite checks all ten cases for axial flattening, transverse widening and preserved perimeter.

**Plastic fence creases (selected metal fence default)**

The optional `components.bending_torque` value selects a connected-section model. Cells within each authored grain remain rigid; neighboring grains are connected by physical rotational joints. Each crease has an elastic rest angle and a yield moment. Its stiffness is yield moment / 0.002 radians, with a damped position motor capped at the yield moment. After each physics substep, angular displacement beyond the elastic allowance updates the permanent rest angle only when the measured motor impulse reaches the yield moment times the substep duration (perfect-plastic return mapping). This prevents incomplete constraint convergence from becoming permanent bending. Sub-yield loads leave that angle unchanged; unloading retains the fold with at most 0.002 radians of elastic recovery. Rest angles and anchors survive unrelated topology rebuilds. The default metal fence uses selected case 3: six bend points at 8 kN m, effective density 780 kg/m³ and friction 0.02. Saved objects retain their authored settings. The comparison varies two to twelve possible bend points across the same free span at the preferred 8 kN m yield moment; these are spatial discretization, not freely rotating hardware hinges. Every point has the same material yield moment. Collision impulses move the sections through Rapier, instead of first pushing against one fixed fence body. The pinned support stays attached. Attached sections do not expire as rubble; explicit cutting can still remove material. This mode bypasses rest-shape plasticity and contact refinement. It currently reports tank path deviation rather than frame strain/plastic-work metrics. Existing plastic-frame materials retain their prior edge-length constraints.
